Yes. Now we're at the **real product-brainstorming stage**.

Before thinking about app screens or technology, let's design the **business flow from “I need something” → “I get the right Pandit/service”**.

And I want to make one important change to our thinking:

> The user should **not necessarily have to know the name of the Puja/service** before using the app.

Someone might type:

> “We are shifting to our new house next Sunday. We want to do some Puja.”

They don't know whether that's Griha Pravesh, Vastu Puja, Ganesh Puja, etc.

**The platform should convert a human request into structured requirements.**

That's the interesting part.

---

# 🔥 Very important: Hard vs Soft requirements

This is something I'd put into the product architecture from the beginning.

### Hard requirement

If it doesn't match, don't show it.

Example:

> Pandit doesn't perform the requested service.

Don't recommend him.

---

### Soft preference

Can be relaxed.

Example:

> User prefers Marathi.

A Hindi/Sanskrit provider might still be shown if the user allows it.

# One more thing: the user doesn't have to start with a Pandit

This is a subtle but important product decision.

The homepage shouldn't scream:

> 🔍 Search a Puja or service

## The key thing I'd lock in from this brainstorming

Our product isn't fundamentally:

> **“Find a Pandit nearby.”**

It is:

> **“Convert a person's real-world religious/ceremony requirement into a transparent, bookable service.”**

And the Pandit is one side of that service.

That distinction gives us room for **home, weddings, corporate events, multiple priests, Samagri, custom ceremonies, NRI bookings, repeat bookings, and eventually a much larger marketplace** without having to redesign the fundamental concept later.
